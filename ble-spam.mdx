![Ble Spam](/images/ble-spam.png "Ble Spam")

A protocol research and security testing tool based on Bluetooth Low Energy advertising (BLE Advertising). Following advertising frame formats published by Apple, Google, Samsung, Microsoft, and other vendors, or reverse-engineered by researchers, it continuously transmits spoofed device-discovery frames at up to +21 dBm with a 20 ms hardware advertising interval. When nearby iPhones, Android phones, Windows 11 laptops, or Galaxy devices are scanning, numerous virtual devices may appear in their Bluetooth lists, or system-level prompts such as “Nearby Pairing,” “Swift Pair,” and “Fast Pair” may appear.

---

# Intended audience

This app is intended for Bluetooth protocol developers, embedded security researchers, and CTF instructors. It can be used to:

- Examine how target devices handle anomalous or high-volume BLE advertisements;
- Reproduce and study the conditions that trigger pairing prompts in publicly known protocols such as Apple Continuity, Google Fast Pair, Microsoft Swift Pair, and Samsung EasySetup;
- Demonstrate that Bluetooth advertisements can be spoofed in IoT environments.

# Basic operation

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open BLE Spam.">
    Open **Hacker Protocol → BLE Spam**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select a mode.">
    Use **Up / Down** to select a mode, then press **A** to open its status page. The app is still idle.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start broadcasting.">
    Press **A** again. The title bar displays **ACTIVE** while broadcasting.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stop or exit.">
    Press **A** on the status page to stop or restart broadcasting. Press **B** to stop and return to the mode list. Press and hold **B** to exit the app.
  </Step>
</Steps>

On the status page, **INT** indicates how often the app updates the test advertisement content, while **PKT** indicates the number of advertisements submitted by the app. Neither value represents the number of advertisements actually detected by a receiving device. Use **Up / Down** to adjust the displayed update interval.

In the current firmware, the menu names for LoveSpouse Play and Stop are **Vibrate 'em All** and **Denial of Pleasure**, respectively.

The open-source project's description of iOS crashes includes version limitations and reports of fixes. Therefore, the official documentation should not say that this mode “supports all iPhones” or “always causes a restart.” [Bluetooth-LE-Spam project notes](https://github.com/simondankelmann/Bluetooth-LE-Spam/blob/main/README.md)

### How to interpret test results

A prompt only shows that the receiving device recognized or displayed an advertisement. It does not prove that pairing succeeded, that the accessory shown in the prompt is real, or that another software version will produce the same result.

Treat **iOS 17 Lockup Crash**, **BT Settings Flood**, the two LoveSpouse modes, and **The Kitchen Sink** as potentially disruptive tests. Stop testing immediately if a device becomes unresponsive or an accessory behaves unexpectedly. Do not use these modes near people or devices outside the scope of your authorization.

---

### Sources and limitations

BLE Spam uses advertising behavior described in public materials or independent research. Some MeowKit modes are derived from the Momentum Apps BLE Spam project. A separate project, Bluetooth-LE-Spam, documents historical platform behavior and fixes in different versions; its results do not guarantee the same effects on MeowKit or current devices.

- [MeowKit firmware implementation](https://github.com/mingolucky/meowkit-s3-firmware/blob/main/src/app/app_07/ble_spam.cpp)
- [Momentum Apps](https://github.com/Next-Flip/Momentum-Apps)
- [Bluetooth-LE-Spam project notes](https://github.com/simondankelmann/Bluetooth-LE-Spam/blob/main/README.md?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The upstream project explicitly documents version limitations for the iOS issue and lists starting and stopping compatible LoveSpouse devices as possible effects. Therefore, the absolute claims that the app “does not modify any data on target devices” and “cannot control devices” were removed from the original draft.
